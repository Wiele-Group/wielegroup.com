WIELE PROPERTIES / THE WIELE VIEW
02 / 16
Growth is visible.
Value needs work.
An investment market is more than a rising price index. The useful question is what an individual
asset can support after costs, risk and the reality of ownership.
Our perspective
Malta enters this review with positive house-price
growth, a larger population and expanding tourism.
Alongside that demand sits a substantial pipeline of
approved dwellings. The combination calls for
selection, rather than a blanket view that every
property will perform alike. [1,4-7]
At Wiele Properties, the starting point is the asset,
the income and the detail. Wiele Properties is your
point of contact in Swieqi, Malta, for property
marketing and introductions. A first apartment and
a wider portfolio deserve the same clarity of
evidence.
This report brings those questions together. It
separates measured results from published
forecasts, and both from our own interpretation
and hypothetical investment tests. There is no
promised yield, assumed stock availability or claim
of a verified sales track record.

The Wiele reading
Price strength and softer transaction volumes can
coexist. One month does not establish a downturn.
Review several months of registrations, achieved
rents and competing completions before changing
the value assigned to a particular asset.
Keep the measures distinct
Annual-average growth describes a whole year.
Year-on-year quarterly growth compares the same
quarter in successive years. Neither is an annual rental
return. Q4 2025 was revised from 6.1% in the original NSO

WIELE PROPERTIES / INCOME / THE DEMAND STORY
05 / 16
Follow the income.
Population and tourism provide context. Collected rent establishes the property income.
AI-created architectural concept. Not an actual Malta property or listing.
Demand is visible
Malta's resident population reached 588,254 at
end-2025, up 2.4%. Net migration was 13,906.
Inbound tourist trips rose 12.9% to 4,022,310, with
nights up 11.0% to 25.4 million. These are demand
indicators, not proof of occupancy or income for a
particular home. [4,5]
Tourist spending reached €3,904.4 million, an
18.6% increase. It includes transport, food and
other expenditure; it cannot be treated as
accommodation revenue. [4]
Rent is not one number
The HICP actual-rent component rose 1.7% on an
annual-average basis in 2025. The Central Bank's
advertised-rent models showed 4.9%-5.3% annual
growth in Q1 2026. Different periods, samples and
definitions make a direct comparison misleading.
[10,11]
The Housing Authority received 48,385 new
contract submissions in 2025. It links the roughly
13% fall partly to registration-rule changes. That
administrative decline cannot be read as an
equivalent fall in demand. [14]
For an acquisition: reconcile signed leases, receipts, arrears and recurring costs. Keep collected, contracted
and projected income separate.

06 / 16
A locality starts
the search.
Wiele research priorities, not a league table of achieved locality returns.
01
Birkirkara / Santa Venera / Qormi
Everyday housing, tested against cost.
Investigate conventional long-let apartments
where an efficient layout and practical access
meet occupier needs. Test the all-in entry cost
against genuinely comparable harbour assets.
Ask for achieved rents, owner-paid costs and competing
completions. Birkirkara had 256 new dwelling approvals in
Q2 2026. [7]
02
Msida / Gżira / Pietà
Compact units. Specific streets.
Assess tenant demand linked to nearby work and
education. A quiet street, ventilation and a usable
layout can matter more to an occupier than a
broad locality label.
Verify street-level rent comparables, damp and
neighbouring works. Msida had 164 Q2 2026 dwelling
approvals. [7]
03
St Paul’s Bay
Activity helps comparison. Income makes the case.
An active transaction market can help an investor
find relevant comparisons. Acquire for
documented income and a defensible entry price,
rather than the activity headline alone.
September 2026 recorded 85 final deeds; Q2 approvals
covered 258 dwellings. Neither figure proves liquidity for
every unit. [7,9]

WIELE PROPERTIES / OPPORTUNITY / THE INDIVIDUAL ASSET
07 / 16
The asset makes
the difference.
A useful location is part of the proposition. The lease, condition and total cost determine the
investment case.
04
Mosta / Naxxar / Marsaskala
Homes for longer tenancies.
Investigate demand for longer tenancies where
parking, lift access and practical room sizes meet
occupier needs. Test whether the specification
supports the achieved rent.
Review comparable leases, service charges, competing
supply and the affordability of a future buyer.
05
Sliema / St Julian’s / eligible SDAs
Read the premium. Check the route.
Consider access, occupier fit and acquisition
eligibility alongside the entry premium. Higher
headline rent does not automatically mean a
higher net return.
Confirm the exact development’s SDA status, service
charges and lawful income use. A locality name alone
proves none of these. [15,16]
06
Gozo / selective locations
Year-round evidence comes first.
Investigate residential or tourism assets with
documented year-round demand. A proven
operation needs its own income record, rather
than an island-wide tourism assumption.
Review winter income, management and access costs,
licences and local comparable rents. Day trips are not
overnight demand.

WIELE PROPERTIES / OPPORTUNITY / BEYOND THE APARTMENT
08 / 16
Beyond the
single apartment.
Blocks, refurbishment and commercial property require a different set of questions.
Income-producing blocks
A block can diversify individual tenant turnover and
simplify control of common areas. Underwrite each
unit separately, including rent collection, lease
expiry, arrears, deposits and deferred works.
Assess the price against concentrated location
exposure and the potential whole-block exit
market.
Refurbishment with a reason
Prioritise work that improves habitability, reduces
maintenance or supports an evidenced rent
increase. Ventilation, waterproofing, electrical
safety and practical layouts deserve consideration
before decorative upgrades. Unapproved
subdivision and extra floors belong in a separate
planning-risk case.
Commercial: start with the lease
For retail, offices and warehouses, review tenant
strength, permitted use, lease breaks, rent-free
periods, fit-out ownership and re-letting costs. A
single departure can remove all operating income.
Residential growth is not a proxy for commercial
performance.

Check the acquisition route
MTCA states that property acquired under an
Acquisition of Immovable Property (AIP) permit cannot
be rented out. Citizenship, residence history, the buyer
entity and the intended use must be assessed for the
actual transaction. [15]
Special Designated Areas (SDAs) have an acquisition
exception described by MTCA. It does not prove that
every development qualifies or that every income use is
permitted. Entities require separate assessment. [16]
Keep tax assumptions visible
Provisional duty of 1% is part of the total duty, not an
extra 1%. MTCA’s example applies 5% final duty, with
market value or transfer value, whichever is higher,
relevant to the tax base. Relief requires confirmation.
[17]
Do not add 18% VAT automatically to the price or rent.
Treatment depends on the transaction and activity.
Allowances here are assumed final cash costs,
including unrecoverable taxes; obtain written
quotations. [20]

The return,
under pressure.
Borrowing can leave little room between operating income and repayments.
€1,094
Base annual cash flow
After debt service and reserve; before tax
-€2,026
Rental-downside cash flow
Same loan; lower income and higher costs
Assume a €180,000 repayment loan: 60% of the purchase price, 25 years and a hypothetical 4.5% annual
rate. Annual repayments are approximately €12,006. The base debt-service coverage ratio is 1.17 times:
€14,100 NOI divided by €12,006 repayments.
Cash equity committed is €167,500: €120,000 price equity, €37,500 costs and works, and a €10,000 liquidity
reserve. The resulting pre-tax cash-on-cash is about 0.65%. No lender offer or current market interest rate
is implied.
At a 6.5% borrowing rate
Annual repayments rise to approximately €14,584. With the base operating income and €1,000 capital
reserve, cash flow falls to about -€1,484 before income tax. The rate is an illustrative stress input.
Tax can consume the remaining cash
MTCA describes an optional 15% final tax on qualifying gross rental income. If the investor qualifies and
elects it, 15% of the base received rent of €17,100 is €2,565. That turns the financed base cash flow into
approximately -€1,471, before any other investor taxes. It is not a complete after-tax return. [19]
The practical consequence: assess the property’s cash resilience, not just the gross yield. Financing fees,
additional works and investor-specific taxes require their own budget.

WIELE PROPERTIES / ACQUISITION / READ THE DETAIL
13 / 16
Buy the asset.
Read the detail.
Public statistics can frame the market. They cannot establish a particular property’s ownership,
condition, lawful use or income.
Before an unconditional commitment
Evidence to obtain
Income and tenants
Signed leases, receipts, deposits, arrears and expiry/break dates;
separate collected and projected income.
Title and rights
Notarial searches, burdens, ground rent, access, airspace, parking and
transfer/use rights.
Planning and condition
Independent architect review of approved plans, permitted use,
structure, damp, waterproofing and services.
Building obligations
Condominium accounts, arrears, insurance, lift liabilities and near-term
major works.
Finance and eligibility
Actual lender terms; acquisition route and intended income use;
investor-specific tax assessment. [15-17]
Exit
Comparable sales, likely buyer pool, sale costs, applicable transfer tax
and an extended holding period.
Price growth is not realised profit
A €300,000 property rising 5% reaches €315,000 before selling costs. That remains below the €337,500
acquisition-and-works outlay in our example, unless the works add separate value. Positive market growth
can coexist with a capital loss on exit.
Deduct outstanding debt, applicable transfer taxes, sale costs, required works and holding-period cash losses from
the proposed exit proceeds. Use the owner’s actual tax basis and holding history.
