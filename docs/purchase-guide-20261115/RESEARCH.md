# International purchase guide — research and release notes

Research edition: 15 November 2026.

Scope: 35 property jurisdictions (four UK nations, EU27, Norway, Switzerland, Monaco, South Africa), plus three regional asset guides (aircraft, yachts, supercars/classic cars). Individual ownership only. No universal legal clearance, tax quote, registry approval or immigration entitlement is asserted.

Country data is the content source of truth in src/data/purchase-jurisdictions.json. Asset data is in src/data/purchase-assets.json. Route files are explicit static routes because the previous deployment exposed a Cloudflare adapter issue with the former parameterised luxury-asset route.

## Evidence boundaries

- Official government, regulator and EU e-Justice sources support the limited checkpoints published. Title-register descriptions do not establish foreign-buyer eligibility.
- The public page displays unresolved items and reminds buyers to obtain advice on current operative local-language law. Latvia explicitly flags an English translation missing March 2026 amendments. Estonia, Greece and Lithuania explicitly leave special-land/nationality questions unresolved.
- Slovenia comparison applies the ministry’s published nationality groups to the UK and South Africa. The inference is identified publicly.
- Spain uses official UK consular guidance; application to other nationals is left for a Spanish practitioner.
- Monaco law 1.598 is limited to its defined Rocher scope and is not presented as all Monaco property law.
- SACAA and SAMSA links identify the authority route; detailed owner-nationality eligibility remains unverified and is disclosed.
- Recommendations for title searches, inspections, insurance, contracts and tax calculations are labelled planning checks, not universal statutory requirements.
- All sources require rechecking before transactional reliance. This is a dated editorial edition, not a monitored legal-advice service.

## Reference-link check

59 unique data-file source URLs were checked. 53 returned HTTP 200. Six rejected or timed out on automated access; no HTTP 404 was returned. Responses are recorded in source-link-check.json. A successful response is a link-health check, not legal verification. Cyprus was read in the browser; other source content was researched through official-page browsing/search. The Dutch page’s automated TLS failure and the Polish ministry timeout do not establish that the pages are unavailable to ordinary visitors.
