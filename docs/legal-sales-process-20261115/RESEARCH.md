# Legal property sales process — 15 November 2026

Expanded the current 35-jurisdiction guide in place: UK four nations, EU27, Norway, Switzerland, Monaco and South Africa. Includes the commitment checkpoint, three/four local stages, document preparation, seller checks, two additional nationality categories, source links, dated scope and visible FAQ answers. Individual direct ownership only; no company, trust, share-sale, immigration or special-asset legal clearance.

## Evidence and method

Live web research used government, registry, legislative and official notarial-body sources. New procedural sources were opened and read where available; blocked material was not treated as a newly verified legal conclusion. The previous guide’s links and scope remain relevant historical research, not proof that every law was re-audited today. `claim-ledger.csv` records each procedural claim, jurisdiction, classification, official URL, authority/provision, effective-date limitation, review date and outstanding work. `country-coverage.csv` retains each commitment/eligibility boundary.

“Sourced requirement” means a specific formality described in the linked authority material, with its stated scope. It does not certify the entire current consolidated law. “Planning check” is advice to obtain an assessment or arrange a step; it is not asserted as universally compulsory. “Confirm locally” remains unresolved. Document lists are preparation checklists, not exhaustive legal lists. The edition date is not a law’s commencement date.

## Material findings applied

- UK: retain the separate national jurisdictions. Ordinary England/Wales and Northern Ireland conveyancing uses exchange; Scottish concluded missives differ. No blanket passport-based purchase clearance added.
- Austria: certified instrument, tax/approval prerequisites and ownership registration; EU/EEA treatment distinguished from provincial third-country/treaty routes.
- Belgium: offers and sales agreements may bind before the authentic deed. Brussels certificates are expressly region-specific, not a uniform Belgian certificate list.
- Croatia: consent/reciprocity and court registration are different checkpoints. Link the current Croatian-language registration page; English instructions can lag.
- Cyprus: DLS page identifies the 12 December 2023 amendment and search certificate within five working days. No outdated stamp-duty amount or universal completion time inserted. Interior Ministry permission page was blocked on direct access; retained as a route with individual confirmation, not newly verified approval.
- Czechia: title by entry, backdated to application receipt; prescribed form and supporting instrument. Do not wrongly say every purchase-agreement signature must be authenticated.
- Denmark: qualifying EU/EEA permanent home is distinct from holiday-home permission; digital deed and settlement obligations are identified.
- Finland: direct land versus housing-company shares; EU/EEA versus Swiss/other nationality; Ministry’s 27 October 2025 report confirms an operative Russian/Belarusian restriction, rather than treating the earlier proposed ban as current law. Detailed exceptions and Åland remain unresolved.
- France: distinguish compromis and unilateral promise. Official public-service page supports a ten-calendar-day non-professional residential withdrawal route; notification, scope and conditions need notarial calculation. Existing-home deed/diagnostics rules are not all property law.
- Germany, Estonia, Netherlands: contract/formal execution and registered ownership are distinct. No notary or escrow requirement invented for other jurisdictions.
- Hungary: current government-office booklet supports EU/EEA/equivalent versus foreign-person permission categories, non-agricultural scope and supporting application evidence. Older register overview does not establish the current electronic process.
- Ireland: CCPC closing, Stamp Duty and Tailte Éireann filing sequence; no universal passport clearance from this source.
- Italy: Revenue Agency supports preliminary-contract and record checks. Older tax examples are not republished as current rates. The Embassy Oslo brochure has questionable immigration/Investor Visa wording; those statements were not used.
- Latvia: corroboration evidence from Land Register Law; current Latvian text, amendments and nationality-specific land eligibility remain unresolved.
- Lithuania: Chamber of Notaries’ 5 February 2026 guidance establishes notarisation and register/capacity checks; land-nationality eligibility remains a separate gap.
- Luxembourg: compromis can bind before the notarial instrument; suspensive finance conditions require explicit drafting.
- Malta: konvenju, 21-day notification, provisional duty of 20% of final duty, final-deed portal documents. EU five-year residence and intended use distinguished from AIP/SDA. No new fixed AIP threshold or all-in tax rate published.
- Poland: Article 92(4) in the linked 2024 Notarial Law consolidation describes same-day electronic filing. Current amendments must be confirmed; exact foreign-permit exemptions remain for counsel. The Ministry page repeatedly timed out and parliamentary page blocked direct access; no claim that these were fully reverified from live body text.
- Portugal: Justice ministry supports obligatory purchase registration and Casa Pronta tax/registration route; promise-contract/default remedies require separate verification.
- Romania, Slovenia: targeted eligibility/registry material informs planning but does not establish a complete current execution and title-transfer code; unresolved steps are labelled.
- Spain: official UK consular guidance says it is under review. Preserve this limitation and obtain Spanish-law confirmation, including military areas and nationality. No announced tax proposal presented as enacted law.
- Sweden: identity/coordination-number requirement from July 2026 and three-month registration. Cooperative interests remain distinct.
- Switzerland: federal residence/permit/UK acquired-rights distinctions; Solothurn procedure explicitly labelled a cantonal example.
- Norway: concession/exemption before deed filing, signatures/consents and D-number route; binding offer rules remain for practitioner confirmation.
- Monaco: previous Rocher-law URL inaccessible on recheck. Prior absolute rule softened and all process stages explicitly unverified. No complete Monaco legal guide claimed.
- South Africa: Cape Town official transfer-document and municipal-clearance example plus SARS non-resident seller withholding. No universal foreign-national mortgage ratio or blanket agricultural eligibility asserted.
