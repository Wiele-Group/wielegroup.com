A well-chosen apartment can give an investor exposure to the visitor economy while retaining a tangible property asset. But the investment case extends well beyond a busy booking calendar. Purchase price, permission to operate, guest capacity and the cost of delivering each stay all shape the result.

The latest evidence supports continued interest in European short lets. It also calls for greater selectivity. For buyers considering Malta alongside other European destinations, the opportunity lies in understanding what a property can lawfully earn, what it costs to run and how it would perform if conditions change.

## Malta’s demand story, in a European context

Guests spent **258.8 million nights** in EU short-stay accommodation booked through Airbnb, Booking and Expedia in the second quarter of 2026, **5.3% more** than a year earlier. Malta recorded growth of **25.0%**, compared with **8.3% in Greece**, **8.1% in Italy**, **2.6% in Portugal** and **1.9% in Spain**. Cyprus declined by **2.4%**. These are changes in guest nights, not property yields or occupancy rates. [Source: Eurostat, Q2 2026 platform accommodation data](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Short-stay_accommodation_offered_via_online_platforms_-_monthly_data).

Malta’s broader tourism figures reinforce the demand picture. NSO recorded approximately **3.60 million inbound tourists in January–September 2026**, with total visitor expenditure of **€3.45 billion**. That expenditure includes the wider visitor economy and must not be read as short-let rental income. [Source: NSO Malta, September 2026 release](https://nso.gov.mt/inbound-tourism-september-2026/).

For investors, the practical question is how much of that demand reaches a particular apartment, at a viable nightly rate, across the whole year. National tourism growth cannot answer that question on its own.

## Buying income: a going concern or a property with potential?

An operating short-let property and an apartment awaiting conversion offer different kinds of evidence. For an established going concern, buyers should seek booking history, accommodation revenue reconciled to receipts, operating costs, cancellation patterns and monthly performance. Confirm what the sale includes: property, furnishings, business assets, contracts and any rights associated with the operation. A trading history does not establish that a licence, platform account or booking book will transfer.

For a property with potential, the income remains a projection until permissions, setup costs and demand have been tested. Works, furnishing, the time needed to begin trading and working capital belong in the acquisition budget. Neither a national growth figure nor a nearby listing’s advertised rate proves achievable income.

Where property and a trading business are sold together, distinguish property income from business profit. A valuation based on revenue, net operating income or business earnings can produce very different answers. The income measure and the assets being acquired must be explicit.

## Housing pressure is changing the investment environment

Tourist accommodation operates within communities that also need homes. CBRE’s midyear European residential review identifies continuing housing shortages and development constraints. BNP Paribas Real Estate’s February review also highlights the combination of supply imbalance and tougher rental regulation. These reports cover broader residential markets; neither establishes the return available from an individual holiday apartment. [CBRE, European Living Outlook: Midyear 2026](https://www.cbre.com/insights/books/european-real-estate-market-outlook-midyear-review-2026/living); [BNP Paribas Real Estate, Residential Market Europe, February 2026](https://www.realestate.bnpparibas.com/media/com/2026-03/FEBRUARY%202026%20Residential%20Europe%20REview.pdf).

The investment implication is straightforward: regulation belongs in the acquisition analysis. A desirable address may face tighter operating conditions, and an existing listing does not prove that its permissions will transfer to a buyer.

## The EU framework increases transparency

**Regulation (EU) 2024/1028 has applied since 20 May 2026.** It provides a common framework for registration and platform data sharing where the relevant national or local systems apply. It does not impose a registration requirement on every short let throughout the EU, and it does not replace local planning or tourism permissions. [Source: European Commission, May 2026 implementation announcement](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/news/new-rules-bring-increased-transparency-short-term-rentals-sector-2026-05-20_en).

Where registration is required, hosts must provide the relevant number, and platforms have checking and reporting duties. Investors should therefore verify the property’s lawful use, registration and operating conditions before treating projected bookings as investable income. [Source: Your Europe, short-term accommodation guidance](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/selling-in-eu/selling-goods-services/short-term-accommodations/index_en.htm).

## Local rules remain decisive

**Malta: a new accommodation framework.** The Tourism Accommodation Regulations took effect on **15 June 2026**. For short-let rented accommodation, the rules require an MTA licence and establish conditions covering planning conformity, insurance, a responsible contact and waste collection. Occupancy is limited to two people per approved bedroom, with a ten-person maximum unless the unit has independent access from a road. These limits can materially change a revenue model built around extra sleeping spaces. [Source: S.L. 409.24, Annex 2, Category E](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/409.24/eng/pdf).

The MTA’s circular also explains phased requirements: existing properties have two years to adapt to air-conditioning requirements, while bedroom-size provisions apply to properties purchased after the regulations came into force. The applicable licence history and transition provisions need checking for each acquisition. [Source: MTA’s 2026 private accommodation circular](https://mta.com.mt/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Circular-regarding-the-New-Tourism-Accommodation-Regulations-2026-Private.pdf).

**France: municipal choices affect operating capacity.** Legislation allows municipalities to reduce the annual tourist-letting limit for a principal residence from 120 to 90 days. This is a local power, not a universal 90-day cap on every French investment property. Buyers must establish the rules for the intended address and use. [Source: French government guidance](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/entreprendre/actualites/A17883?profil=tout).

**Spain: current legal text matters.** A Supreme Court judgment of 19 May 2026 annulled provisions of the state’s unified registration procedure, including the specified obligation to obtain a number through the property or movable-property registry. Earlier summaries of the 2025 system therefore require care; regional and municipal tourist-accommodation requirements still need separate examination. [Source: published Supreme Court ruling](https://www.boe.es/buscar/doc.php?id=BOE-A-2026-12300).

Spain’s October 2026 decree-law also schedules a VAT change from **1 December 2026** for specified furnished stays of up to 30 nights, with a principal-residence exception. Its continued legislative status and application must be checked before pricing an acquisition or a stay. [Source: Real Decreto-ley 29/2026, Article 7](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/rdl/2026/10/06/29/con).

## Revenue is only the starting point

A short let is an operating business attached to a property. Platform charges, guest services, cleaning, utilities, insurance, repairs and replacement furnishings all compete for the same rental receipts. Financing, tax and major refurbishment sit beyond those day-to-day costs.

Consider a deliberately hypothetical example: an apartment achieving €100 per occupied night, 65% annual occupancy, variable costs of 25% of accommodation revenue and €8,000 in annual fixed operating costs. It produces approximately **€23,725 in revenue** and **€9,794 in operating surplus**, before financing, tax and capital expenditure. At 55% occupancy, that surplus falls to approximately **€7,056**—a reduction of about **28%**.

This is why a credible appraisal needs a monthly income model, a complete cost schedule and a reserve for disruption. Yield should be assessed against the full acquisition and setup cost, with its gross or net basis stated clearly.

Borrowing also deserves a fresh calculation. The ECB kept its deposit facility rate at **2.50% on 29 October 2026** and did not commit to a future rate path. A policy rate is not a mortgage offer: the investor’s actual rate, fees, repayment structure and refinancing exposure determine the financing burden. [Source: ECB monetary policy decision](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2026/html/ecb.mp261029~9a493adf17.en.html).

## The outlook for 2027–2028: selective opportunity

Our reading of the evidence is that Europe’s short-let investment market will remain active, with greater separation between well-prepared properties and assets whose economics rely on optimistic assumptions. This is a conditional editorial outlook, not a numerical forecast of prices, yields or returns.

The European Travel Commission’s Q2 review describes resilient tourism alongside economic and geopolitical uncertainty. That supports a balanced view: demand remains meaningful, while household spending, travel disruption and operating costs can change the income available to owners. [Source: ETC, European Tourism Q2 2026, public summary](https://etc-corporate.org/reports/european-tourism-trends-prospects-q2-2026/).

Three outcomes frame the next 12–24 months:

- **Central case:** visitor demand remains supportive, but individual results depend on local supply, lawful capacity, pricing and cost control.
- **Stronger case:** demand grows faster than competing accommodation supply and permissions remain stable, allowing some operators to improve income.
- **Weaker case:** softer travel spending, higher costs or tighter operating rules reduce cash flow and expose purchases made on aggressive projections.

Another development to watch is the EU’s proposed **Affordable Housing Act**. The Commission’s October explanation describes a framework for assessing certain housing-related restrictions, including short lets. It remains a proposal under consideration by Parliament and Council; it is not an enacted EU-wide letting ban or night cap. [Source: European Commission, proposal explanation, 23 October 2026](https://housing.ec.europa.eu/affordable-housing-act/affordable-housing-act-understanding-proposed-framework_en).

The strongest counterargument to the opportunity is that tourism growth can be absorbed by new accommodation supply, leaving individual owners with little pricing power. Evidence of declining local occupancy, slower bookings or rising compliance costs would weaken the outlook. Stable permissions, verified off-season demand and resilient income after costs would strengthen it.
