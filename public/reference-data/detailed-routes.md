# Detailed purchase and sale routes

Wiele Investments · UK, European Union and South Africa · 21 November 2026

Use alongside the 27-entry matrix. Local professional confirmation is required for the actual asset, parties, funding, legal use and transaction date.

## UK · property

Choose the UK nation before costing the purchase

1. Identify the nation, title number, freehold/leasehold interest, legal owner, tenancy, entity control and proposed use. Ask for the actual lease, title burdens, service accounts or planning records where applicable.
2. Have the conveyancer review an offer, auction or reservation before signature. In an ordinary private-treaty transaction England and Wales use exchange of contracts. In Scotland the contract becomes binding upon conclusion of missives. In Northern Ireland check the local exchange/completion procedure.
3. Complete searches, survey/valuation and mortgage approval. Verify funding and the solicitor’s account through an established channel. Negotiate conditions for unresolved title, lease, survey and finance issues.
4. Exchange or conclude missives through the appropriate professionals, then complete/settle and obtain dated delivery and discharge evidence. The practitioner files the local purchase tax return and title application. Reconcile completion statement and registration separately.

**Buyer.** Record worldwide existing property interests, qualifying dwelling use, purchase price, additional-property and residence tests. Confirm joint-purchaser/entity rules and relevant reliefs.

**Seller.** Provide title and seller information, lease pack or home report where appropriate, authority to sign, redemption statements and residential tax position. Ask the adviser whether a non-resident disposal return is due within 60 days of completion.

**Domestic.** Citizenship alone does not establish the SDLT residence result. Check prior ownership, replacement-main-home and locally applicable reliefs.

**EU/EEA.** Check ownership vehicle, residence, immigration and lender eligibility independently. An EEA passport does not establish SDLT residence.

**Other foreign / non-resident.** On an England/Northern Ireland residential purchase, test the non-UK-resident surcharge. An overseas entity may need a Companies House Overseas Entity ID, verified beneficial ownership and current update statement before certain acquisitions or dispositions.

**Financial or customs rule.** England and Northern Ireland use SDLT. Where applicable add two percentage points to the residential rates and assess additional-dwelling rules separately. Wales uses Land Transaction Tax and Scotland uses Land and Buildings Transaction Tax. Use their own bands and supplements.

**Hold point.** Hold unconditional commitment if title, funds or an entity restriction would prevent registration or delivery.

**Responsible professional.** Local conveyancer or Scottish solicitor, lender, land register and tax adviser.

## EU · property

Choose the member state, region and parcel

1. Confirm land-register extract, cadastral parcel and what transfers. Check land versus company shares, usufruct, agricultural interests, occupancy, heritage, coastal or military-zone controls and permitted use. Map purchaser/seller beneficial ownership.
2. Obtain a national eligibility opinion for the exact persons/control structure and parcel. Check permits, reciprocity, pre-emption, family consents, assets held abroad and sanctions where relevant. Get planning and rental-use confirmation independently.
3. Have local counsel identify the first binding act and applicable withdrawal period. Arrange independent title/planning evidence and specify financing, permit, due-diligence and deposit-return conditions before it.
4. Use the locally required instrument, authenticated signatures, notary or solicitor. Agree debt discharge and protected settlement. File the correct taxes and record the transfer using the national system. Obtain register proof and document local tax and occupancy obligations.

**Buyer.** Ask for national transfer taxes, VAT for applicable new/commercial supplies, municipal surcharges, professional fees and ownership costs. Use the actual municipality, currency, taxpayer and transaction date.

**Seller.** Document title, authority, energy/planning/tenancy and prior capital costs. Check pre-emption, seller capital-gains return, retention or non-resident withholding and debt releases in that country.

**Domestic.** Local ownership and residence can simplify some admission tests. Parcel use and special land rules still need checking.

**EU/EEA.** Apply EU equal-treatment rules to the covered circumstances. Record the specific EU/EEA country and any EEA, Swiss or treaty rights. Some permanent-home and holiday-home regimes differ.

**Other foreign / non-resident.** Assess actual citizenships, protected status, residence and beneficial control. Prioritise reciprocity, permits, national security and restricted land. UK nationals generally require a separate third-country/treaty analysis after Brexit.

**Financial or customs rule.** An EU country’s domestic rules set the applicable rate and base. Residence, new versus used status, share deal, commercial activity and local authority can change the result.

**Hold point.** Hold commitment where a binding preliminary contract would precede a needed permit or verified escape clause.

**Responsible professional.** National notary/solicitor, registry, competent land authority and tax adviser.

## ZA · property

Structure the offer, funding and deed transfer in South Africa

1. Obtain deed and registered bond details, seller authority, property description, title conditions, occupancies, zoning and municipal/sectional-title or estate obligations. Verify the practitioner or firm’s current standing when applicable.
2. Review the written offer to purchase before acceptance. Agree finance, sale-of-existing-property, inspection, fixtures, occupational rent, deposit recipient and deadlines. Validate seller and purchaser identity and beneficial ownership through the appointed professional.
3. Ask the authorised dealer how inbound funds and funding source will be recorded. Keep proof of remittance, exchange records and acquisition costs for a later repatriation assessment. Obtain the lender’s case-specific facility offer.
4. Conveyancer obtains guarantees, bond cancellation documents, municipal clearance and any levy/HOA clearances and compliance certificates that apply. Arrange SARS duty/VAT treatment and any section 35A retention before lodging linked documents at the Deeds Office.
5. After registration reconcile guarantees, seller settlement, retentions and permitted remittance against bank and tax records. Confirm any subsequent tax filing.

**Buyer.** Identify natural person/entity and any joint purchasers, matrimonial property regime, SA tax residence and exchange-control status. Separate the purchase from permission to reside or operate a business.

**Seller.** Declare tax residence. Supply existing bonds, rates and levy clearances, applicable disclosure/certificates, original acquisition expenses and the authorised-dealer trail. Arrange a SARS directive where justified.

**Domestic.** Check VAT versus transfer duty and the lender’s ordinary credit approval. SA citizenship can coexist with non-resident tax or exchange-control status.

**EU/EEA.** EU/EEA nationality brings no independent South African funding or tax exemption. Record actual residence and inward funding.

**Other foreign / non-resident.** SARB I.1(E) generally applies 1:1 local financing to non-resident residential/commercial acquisitions. One rand of relevant introduced/owned cash or assets can support an equivalent borrowing base subject to the manual and lender. I.1(E)(ii) permits normal lending criteria for non-residents living and working in SA acquiring residential property. Request the authorised dealer’s written category and evidence requirements.

**Financial or customs rule.** For applicable transfers from 1 April 2026 SARS retained its prior duty schedule: R0 through R1.21m, then marginal bands of 3%, 6%, 8%, 11% and 13%. Section 35A applies when aggregate consideration exceeds R2m and seller is non-resident: generally 7.5% natural person, 10% company, 15% trust on qualifying consideration, subject to directive. This is an advance payment. Purchaser remits within 14 days if resident or 28 if non-resident after withholding.

**Hold point.** Never turn a 1:1 funding rule into an automatic mortgage offer, or assume a generic attestation suffices. Keep funds and identity documents in the appointed professional’s restricted system.

**Responsible professional.** Conveyancer, bond attorneys, authorised dealer, SARS and Deeds Office.

## UK · yacht

Check title, flag and UK customs status separately

1. Identify hull/build number, registered owner, type of registry, last ports, prior importer/exporter, any lease/charter arrangements and whether the delivery port is in Great Britain or Northern Ireland.
2. Obtain title chain, registry transcript and mortgage releases. Confirm proposed ownership and register part directly with the flag. Agree inventory, survey, sea trial, refundable deposit, acceptance notice, reinspection and long-stop.
3. Document the prospective customs event with the broker before movement. Obtain original VAT/import, free-circulation and export evidence. A UK flag alone cannot establish payment history. Check whether an onward import or sale offshore changes relief.
4. Execute bill of sale, mortgage discharge, acceptance and delivery protocols against verified funds. File ownership/flag change and arrange insurance, crew competence and safety certificates. Before any charter, verify operating permissions and the applicable commercial code.

**Buyer.** Use an independent surveyor and marine lawyer. Obtain register confirmation, survey scope and an insurance binder. Identify passenger count, operating area and commercial use.

**Seller.** Disclose liabilities, registration, prior VAT/import, warranties, place of sale, service records and mortgage payoff/discharge arrangements.

**Domestic.** UK residence/citizenship can affect flag route or temporary use. Show proof of qualification and VAT history independently.

**EU/EEA.** An EU owner moving a yacht to Great Britain should establish private temporary use or permanent import. Confirm NI/EU treatment separately.

**Other foreign / non-resident.** For a new owner importing a yacht bought outside the UK, check import VAT at entry and the actual importer’s relief entitlement. Request written assessment before delivery.

**Financial or customs rule.** Under HMRC returned-goods guidance, establish earlier UK free circulation and no more than running repairs abroad. Import VAT relief generally requires the same exporter and re-importer. A new offshore purchaser cannot inherit that VAT relief on the seller’s export. Ask HMRC about timing, vessel-specific relaxations and evidence.

**Hold point.** Hold delivery until lender release, customs exposure and registry eligibility are dealt with in the settlement instructions.

**Responsible professional.** Flag registry, marine lawyer, customs adviser, surveyor and commercial certifying authority where relevant.

## EU · yacht

Establish Union customs status and permitted use

1. Identify physical location and customs territory at sale/delivery, existing flag and legal/beneficial owner, person established as importer/operator, normal residence and planned cruising/charter.
2. Trace invoice, import declaration, VAT accounting and vessel movements. Seek dated Union-status evidence where appropriate. Compare permanent import with conditional temporary admission and identify the responsible customs office.
3. Obtain flag eligibility, transcript, mortgages and title chain. Agree survey, sea trial, deposit/notice conditions and place of delivery. For charter obtain written coastal-state, flag, VAT and crewing advice before projecting income.
4. Complete bill of sale and discharge, delivery and acceptance. File required import/re-export, ownership, safety and charter documents and retain receipts and log evidence.

**Buyer.** Determine whether you intend private pleasure use, transfer or charter. Obtain evidence of the person permitted to operate the vessel under the selected customs procedure.

**Seller.** Supply proof of customs status, title, discharge and prior use. If the yacht is under temporary admission, consult customs before sale, transfer of use or delivery.

**Domestic.** An EU resident’s ownership or EU flag does not by itself evidence Union status. Confirm import/payment and movement documentation.

**EU/EEA.** EU citizenship and EEA residence describe different tests. Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein are outside the EU customs territory. Verify normal residence, establishment and actual customs route.

**Other foreign / non-resident.** For qualifying private non-Union pleasure craft, normal temporary admission is up to 18 months. Check overseas registration/ownership, qualifying use and establishment. Movement between member states does not restart the clock. Re-export or discharge correctly. Exceptions for EU-resident operators and extensions have specific conditions.

**Financial or customs rule.** Temporary admission suspends duty/VAT subject to compliance. It does not grant a general right to sell, let or charter. Budget destination import VAT/duty if entering free circulation. Obtain state-specific supply/charter treatment.

**Hold point.** Hold charter promises or handover under temporary admission until customs and maritime authorities confirm the permitted procedure.

**Responsible professional.** Entry customs office, flag registry, coastal authority, marine lawyer and surveyor.

## ZA · yacht

Coordinate the port, SAMSA and SARS before sailing or delivery

1. Describe vessel identity, internationally recognised flag, owner/control, build and tonnage, category/use, crew, last port, delivery port and intended duration. Ask the port/SARS and immigration contacts for its inbound procedure.
2. Have a maritime professional check registrable ownership, transfer paperwork, any existing marine mortgages, flag-change/deletion arrangements, seaworthiness/survey and local operating limits. Obtain written SAMSA route where South African registration is proposed.
3. Distinguish visiting foreign-flag pleasure craft from permanent import and domestic/commercial charter operation. Secure the customs classification, valuation, importer authority and transport documents for the actual route.
4. Execute bill of sale, mortgage release, delivery and acceptance with insurance effective at risk transfer. Obtain customs clearance plus registry/operating and crew endorsements before commercial employment. Keep closure or departure evidence.

**Buyer.** Ask for chain of title, registered mortgage search, release, condition/survey and insurance terms. Appoint a customs broker for permanent import.

**Seller.** Document tax/import status, port clearance, seller authority, mortgages, sea-trial authority and permission to deliver clear title.

**Domestic.** Confirm local registration/small-vessel listing, safety equipment, skipper competency and commercial status with SAMSA for the specific vessel.

**EU/EEA.** EU/EEA title or coding alone does not grant SA flag or passenger/charter permission. Ask SAMSA about recognition and owner qualification.

**Other foreign / non-resident.** Foreign-flag visiting owners should obtain entry and re-export/clearance instructions. For a permanent import or sale, confirm customs duty/VAT, eligible owner and any company/control structure before changing possession.

**Financial or customs rule.** Use a written landed-cost assessment and distinguish port dues, agent fees, VAT and tariff duties. Evidence of tax paid abroad cannot automatically discharge SA customs liabilities.

**Hold point.** Current SAMSA documentary/eligibility detail for the exact owner, operation and vessel must be confirmed by the registrar.

**Responsible professional.** SAMSA registry/survey office, port authority, SARS customs, immigration and maritime practitioner.

## UK · classic-car

Resolve tariff classification before committing to landed cost

1. Match vehicle to factory records and chassis/engine numbers, photographs of stampings and registration/export documents. Check history of replacement parts, replicas, restomods, title/finance and seller authority.
2. Record actual origin and dispatch, GB/NI delivery route, prior registration, commodity classification and value including transport/insurance as legally required. If classification is material and uncertain, obtain the proper customs ruling before shipment.
3. Agree condition inspection, deposit return, enclosed carrier, export formalities and insurance. Consider UK cultural-object export licensing for qualifying outbound vehicles. A carrier doing a bonded movement must arrange the correct customs transit and destination discharge.
4. Complete customs entry, settle applicable duty/VAT and arrange NOVA notification generally within 14 days for a permanent UK arrival. Complete vehicle approval where required, DVLA registration, insurance and statutory roadworthiness before road use.

**Buyer.** Distinguish historic registration/road tax, competition/passport documentation and customs classification. Originality, material changes, type production and recognised historic/racing significance need evidence.

**Seller.** Provide sale invoice with parties and VIN, title/lien releases, registration information, historical chain, transport instructions and evidence of permanent export when applicable.

**Domestic.** UK ownership does not remove import taxes when acquiring a non-UK vehicle. Existing domestic cars follow the domestic registration/title procedure.

**EU/EEA.** EU dispatch and EU goods origin are distinct. Check applicable UK/EU origin documentation and GB versus NI customs rules before assuming preferential duty.

**Other foreign / non-resident.** Determine importer and intended use. Temporary admission, permanent registration and use at a competition require separate advice. Provide physical identifiers and prior registration documents.

**Financial or customs rule.** HMRC links qualifying collector vehicles to heading 9705. The reduced UK valuation for qualifying collector articles gives effective 5% import VAT. Age alone does not secure it. Obtain the entry-date tariff measure, valuation and classification. Illustratively £100,000 qualifying VAT basis produces £5,000 effective import VAT before any excluded costs or fees.

**Hold point.** Hold any all-in cost claim where classification, origin or the DVLA identity route remains unresolved.

**Responsible professional.** Customs agent/HMRC, independent marque expert and DVLA.

[HMRC · collectors’ vehicle classification](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/classifying-vehicles) · [HMRC · valuing goods for import VAT](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/how-to-value-goods-for-import-vat) · [HMRC · notifying vehicle arrivals](https://www.gov.uk/importing-vehicles-into-the-uk/telling-hmrc) · [DVLA · registering imports](https://www.gov.uk/importing-vehicles-into-the-uk/registering-an-imported-vehicle) · [UK government · take cultural objects abroad](https://www.artscouncil.org.uk/supporting-arts-museums-and-libraries/export-controls)

## EU · classic-car

Apply the entry country’s customs, VAT and road-registration rules

1. Confirm where the car is in free circulation, proposed import/customs office, buyer’s residence, seller’s dealer/private status, origin, odometer, first use, model production status and original component evidence.
2. For third-country entry use tariff classification at the entry date. Chapter 97 additional note on heading 9705 addresses original state without substantial change, age of at least 30 years and discontinued model/type, with specific historic-event/racing provisions. A restomod or replica may require ordinary vehicle classification.
3. For an intra-EU transfer of Union goods, review intra-EU VAT instead of import duty. Check whether the car is a new means of transport for VAT under the 6 months/6,000 km test. For a used car both more than six months and more than 6,000 km are required. Examine the dealer’s invoice/margin scheme.
4. Verify national road-permission, steering configuration, approval, registration taxes, historic status, technical inspection and insurance. Obtain sale, export/deregistration and enclosed transit documents, pay the applicable amounts and document arrival and registration.

**Buyer.** Ask local registration authority about conformity, original registration papers and acceptability of modifications. Obtain legal title evidence and marque authentication independently.

**Seller.** Supply original registration paperwork, proof of sale, VAT treatment, title and finance releases, provenance and dispatch/export evidence. Establish whether cultural-heritage export controls apply.

**Domestic.** Domestic historic registration is state-specific and distinct from EU customs classification. Verify authority requirements before resale and road use.

**EU/EEA.** EU/EEA citizenship is separate from Union-goods status and the normal-residence registration country. An EEA-country dispatch can involve a customs border.

**Other foreign / non-resident.** Seek importer registration and clearance at the actual border. Confirm duty, import VAT and nation-specific reduced rates or registration charges. A UK effective 5% assumption cannot price an EU import.

**Financial or customs rule.** Customs classification, country of origin, origin preference and import VAT rate are separate decisions. An auction result may include buyer premium and taxes that need separate treatment in the customs value.

**Hold point.** Require written ruling or qualified broker assessment if vehicle identity, historic classification, road eligibility or transit closure is decisive.

**Responsible professional.** National customs and registration offices, EU TARIC/BTI channel and specialist inspector.

## ZA · classic-car

Secure import and road eligibility before shipping

1. Identify applicant citizenship/residence category, intended permanent/temporary/race use, right/left-hand drive, previous SA registration, condition, originality and prior ownership. Treat a purchase or family gift as an import if entering SA permanently.
2. Apply to ITAC under the right used-vehicle route. The published IE463 vintage/collector route is for SA citizens/nationals; the vintage category describes 40 years or older. International collector consideration and supporting affidavit are separate from age alone. Different immigration or returning-resident routes have their own conditions.
3. Obtain NRCS letter of authority or a case-specific written exemption for the intended use. Inspect the vehicle and retrieve factory, chassis/engine and registration documents. Confirm road registration separately, particularly for left-hand drive.
4. Appoint transporter and broker after required permissions. Secure export documents, customs valuation, duties/levies/VAT and permitted delivery. Where using bonded transit, verify guarantee, authorised premises and acquittal. For IE463, separately consider the declaration about not sourcing the car from a bonded warehouse.
5. Obtain customs release, local vehicle identity/police and registration requirements, certificate(s), insurance and roadworthiness. Observe disposal restrictions, including the two-year restriction stated for this ITAC vintage/collector pathway.

**Buyer.** Require written permit route and registration decision for this vehicle/importer. A foreign buyer cannot assume the SA-national IE463 route.

**Seller.** Supply bill of sale, unencumbered ownership, VIN evidence, export papers, history and local permits. Verify any restrictions from an earlier permit before agreeing an onward sale.

**Domestic.** SA buyer acquiring abroad: check citizen/national, returning-resident or other applicable category. A car already legally registered locally follows a different path.

**EU/EEA.** EU/EEA nationality and a European road certificate do not establish eligibility to import/register permanently. Determine immigration status or other specific permit category.

**Other foreign / non-resident.** Identify establishment and purpose. A temporary visitor or competition entry requires the applicable customs/temporary conditions. Permanent road use requires separate authorisation.

**Financial or customs rule.** NRCS import guidance describes documented registration in the Republic in the relevant owner/importer’s name before 1 January 2000 for the LHD route it addresses. A 2010 transport ministry note describes resale/licensing for certain already-licensed LHD stock before 23 July 2004. Obtain written VIN-specific confirmation for the exact import, domestic resale or public-road scenario. Neither date is an age-based blanket import entitlement.

**Hold point.** Hold shipment and price representations until ITAC, NRCS and registration authority positions, permitted purpose and declaration restrictions are reconciled.

**Responsible professional.** ITAC, NRCS, SARS customs, licensing authority and insured specialist carrier.
