Provenance and inspection

A factory archive extract explains how the car left the works. A specialist must also inspect today’s physical stampings, material and assembly. “Matching numbers” should specify exactly which components were compared.

Record 01 · Factory identity
Compare chassis, body, engine block, cylinder head and gearbox against the factory archive. Inspect the stamped surfaces and rivets, retain dated photographs and document replacement components. No genuine certificate has been issued for this example.
Record 02 · Restoration
Mock 2014–2016 restoration ledger covers sill repairs, bare-shell repaint and replacement upholstery. The example lacks photographs of the inner floor seams before closure. Request an endoscope inspection.
Record 03 · Service history
Example 2021 entry covers cooling hoses and brake hydraulics. Example 2024 entry covers carburettor balancing and differential seals. Check dates and mileages against original invoices and the supplier, with permission.
Record 04 · Condition
Example issues: minor oil seep at rear of engine and ageing rear tyres. Commission a cold start, compression/leak-down test, chassis and sill inspection, brake test and check the age codes on all tyres.
Record 05 · FIA and concours
Scenario has no FIA Historic Technical Passport, contemporary competition entry, judged concours result or class award. Ask for event entry lists, period photos and result sheets before using any such claim.
Before release
Check V5C identity against chassis details and separate ownership evidence, search for outstanding finance, agree transport and collection insurance, then exchange cleared funds and signed sale receipt.

Observed sale and illustrative value

Iconic Auctioneers published a £74,250 result on 14 November 2026 for Lot 134, a UK-market 1966 Series I 4.2 coupé. It is a separate car. Review condition, auction premium basis and restoration invoices before reconciling with a private asking price.

Example asking price
£82,000, about 10.4% above the £74,250 published result. This spread is the difference between an illustrative ask and one historical sale.
Comparator exclusions
Different body, market or scope materially changes the comparison. Exclude 2+2 cars, roadsters and unfinished projects unless a defensible adjustment is documented. A single sale cannot establish historical market growth.
Five-year gross scenarios
Compounding £82,000 at −3%, 0% and +4% annually gives exit prices of £70,416, £82,000 and £99,766. Rates are assumptions for testing retention value. There is no asserted historical appreciation curve.
Ownership cost
Annual storage £1,800 + insurance £900 + maintenance reserve £2,800 = £5,500. Add illustrative pre-purchase inspection £750 and enclosed UK transport £650. Major restoration, overseas freight, finance and taxes excluded.
Net result after five years
At +4% annual appreciation, £99,766 less 5% assumed selling costs, £27,500 running costs, £1,400 entry costs and £82,000 acquisition = approximately −£16,123. Nominal break-even requires about £116,737 before selling costs, equivalent to 7.32% annual price growth.
Additional market evidence
Assemble at least several recent comparable sales with known currency, premium, specification, steering, body and condition. Calculate any observed repeat-sale CAGR only from a matched vehicle with dated prices and documented intervening restoration.

UK retention, EU delivery and South African import

Road registration, taxation, historic competition eligibility and originality each require their own evidence. The example is located in Great Britain.

UK buyer
Use a signed sale receipt for ownership, notify DVLA of the keeper change, and arrange tax, roadworthy condition and insurance before driving. Confirm historic-vehicle tax/MOT eligibility, including relevant substantial modifications.
Buyer importing into an EU member state
Record permanent export from Great Britain, customs value, transport and origin evidence. Obtain the destination authority’s customs classification and VAT treatment. Verify local technical inspection, RHD registration and lighting adjustments before shipment.
South African buyer
Obtain an ITAC permit and an applicable NRCS letter of authority or documented authority decision before shipping. Collector status, age and RHD configuration alone do not confer import or road-registration approval. Confirm SARS charges, clearance, registration and transport insurance.
Car returning to Great Britain
Check import VAT/duty treatment and the exact heading with HMRC before using a relief estimate. Originality, historical condition, age and whether the type is still produced matter to a heading 9705 assessment. A UK return may have a different relief route.
Seller and handover
Separate title, V5C keeper record, chassis verification, export notification and destination import. Inventory history papers, keys, tools and spares. Agree who bears freight, customs storage and insurance risk at each handover.
